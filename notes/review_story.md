Reviewed 2026-10-14: `main.tex`, `sections/abstract.tex`, `sections/introduction.tex` and `sections/results.tex`, against `AGENTS.md`, `notes/source_reuse.md`, the verified simulation literature notes and the refreshed export. This is a narrative review; Methods and figures remain deferred. Line pointers refer to the reviewed draft. No manuscript source was edited.

## Overall assessment

The integrated argument is coherent for an NBT submission: a configurable cellular task distribution teaches useful inference from limited labelled context, supports a compact specialist, and improves existing tabular learners. The cross-backbone result is the strongest evidence that the contribution extends beyond a particular architecture. Transcriptome-scale input and faithful, affordable query-gene explanations make the resulting method useful for cellular analysis. The provisional disease application can develop the biological endpoint, but the current red status correctly keeps it outside the established evidence.

The draft needs targeted corrections and compression, rather than a new story. Its main imbalance is that computational qualifications sometimes receive more prose than the biological use of the method. Preserve the necessary boundaries, but attach each boundary to its claim instead of repeatedly closing paragraphs with general disclaimers.

## Corrections before the next prose pass

1. **Distinguish the shared generator from identical training distributions.** The abstract's “same prior”, introduction P-I05 (`sections/introduction.tex:14`) and Results P-R09–10 (`sections/results.tex:34,37`) imply identical cellular exposure. The export explicitly states that 83.3% of CellPFN's scheduled tasks came from prior v1 and 16.7% from v2.2-B; all simulated cells in the tabular adaptation came from v2.2-B (`../paper_export_20261011/FIGURES.md:140,396,1709`). Use “the cellular prior framework” or “the cellular task generator” where appropriate, and add one short exposure-history clause to P-R03. In P-R09 identify adaptation as using the final prior configuration. This preserves the reusable-prior claim while preventing the fidelity analysis of v2.2-B from being read as an explanation of all CellPFN training.

2. **Put transfer directly after competitive performance.** Move P-R09–10, currently after the gene-scaling subsection, immediately after P-R04–06. This follows the agreed sequence in `AGENTS.md`: useful biological inference with limited training-task exposure, then transfer to pretrained tabular models. The one-shot gains of 7.3, 10.8 and 5.6 percentage points answer why the prior is of interest beyond CellPFN. Scaling should follow as a practical property of the specialist. No new subsection or evidence is needed.

3. **Keep the training-exposure claim explicit in the abstract and title.** P-R03 correctly distinguishes scheduled episodes, reused banks and nonzero-loss episodes, and rules out a matched-compute interpretation. The abstract currently converts the TabICLv2 count into “training episodes”, whereas the cited quantity is synthetic pretraining datasets. Prefer an explicit comparison: “after 384,000 scheduled synthetic episodes, compared with approximately 35 million synthetic datasets reported for TabICLv2.” A ratio can remain if the units remain visible. The title's generic “efficient” (`main.tex:23`) has no specific efficiency measure; “A cellular prior enables transferable single-cell inference with CellPFN” is clearer unless the title explicitly names the supported efficiency axis. This is a wording correction, not a recommendation to remove limited exposure from the story.

4. **Name the biological application at the level currently measured.** The final Results heading (`sections/results.tex:58`) says “cell states”, but P-R16 currently reports donor-level neuropathology discrimination. Use “A provisional application examines neuropathology-associated expression” until the cellular-state analysis exists. Keep O02–O04 red as required. O03's “will establish whether” (`sections/results.tex:64`) can become “will test whether”; future work cannot guarantee a conclusive answer. O04 remains an explicitly intended endpoint, not a completed result.

## Simulator motivation and literature fit

The introduction successfully recovers the most valuable legacy motivations: donor diversity rather than cell count (P-I01), known labels and controlled variation (P-I04), and transfer as the test of prior utility (P-I04–05). It does not revive unsupported claims of universal fidelity or unique donor simulation. The original passages marked `\phflo` are indexed in `notes/source_reuse.md:20–22`; those markings should not be presented as proof of handwritten authorship.

One replacement would sharpen the reference-fit motivation without adding length. Replace “For PFN training, simulation has a further role...” in P-I04 with: “For PFN training, the design objective extends beyond reproducing a reference dataset to generating diverse labelled inference problems.” This captures the legacy argument without implying that Splatter, scDesign2/3 or other existing tools lack configurable parameters. The next sentence already explains what is varied, so no additional simulator rationale is necessary.

The simulator citations fit their claims. Splatter/SymSim/scDesign2/3 cover statistical and process-based modelling; splatPop supports donor/cohort variation; SERGIO/dyngen/scMultiSim support regulatory or mechanistic scope. cscGAN and scDiffusion support learned conditional synthesis, and “learned generative models” would distinguish them more clearly from other reference-fitted statistical simulators. FlowNet and Wood support the short synthetic-to-real precedent. Crowell and Duo support multidimensional fidelity and evaluation boundaries; they do not themselves demonstrate PFN transfer, which correctly remains this paper's empirical claim. The paragraph is citation-dense: retaining representative citations for each stated family and moving remaining coverage to the later Methods/Discussion would preserve thorough research without making the introduction a catalogue.

## Prose economy and paragraph necessity

- **Abstract:** consider exchanging the parameter count for the 5.6–10.8-point range of one-shot adaptation gains. The latter conveys the reusable prior's value; the model size is already prominent in Results. Keep the exposure count because it is part of the intended central claim.
- **P-I05 (`introduction.tex:14`):** its final sentence repeats the preceding paragraph's transfer criterion. End after the concrete contributions, or replace the sentence with one concise biological-use statement.
- **P-R02 (`results.tex:7`):** remove “The current model produces direct neural predictions; no analytic classifier supplies its starting logits.” This resolves a historical architecture distinction that the new reader has not encountered. The support-conditioned computation and absence of target-task gradient updates are the useful points here.
- **P-R03 (`results.tex:10`):** remove the final rhetorical setup. The following subsection directly answers the question, and the exposure comparison already establishes the motivation.
- **P-R05 (`results.tex:18`):** the six-model scoreboard can be reduced to CellPFN plus the comparisons needed to establish its competitive range. Preserve the complete values in the evidence record and eventual figure, rather than forcing every comparator into this paragraph. P-R06's two contrasting biological regimes are useful and should stay.
- **P-R10 (`results.tex:37`):** delete “They do not require that the same architecture be optimal for both uses.” It adds no result or necessary qualification.
- **P-R11–12 (`results.tex:39–45`):** the heading currently promises a covariance result but also contains task-selection and simulator-diagnostic evidence. Rename to “Prior and architecture changes have regime-dependent effects”, or retain the operator heading and relocate the simulator diagnostic sentence to the prior description. Keep the failed prespecified operator result visible; prose economy does not justify hiding it.
- **P-R14 (`results.tex:53`):** the 10–13-forward-pass cost is sufficient for the main narrative alongside fidelity. The 67.9-second versus extrapolated 61-hour example and shared-GPU qualification can move together to the eventual figure legend or Methods. Preserve the selected-gene/correct-cell scope of the fidelity check in the main result.

These changes retain the evidence boundaries while making the progression easier to follow: biological prediction, reusable prior, transcriptome-scale inference, design limits, gene-level interpretation and the explicitly provisional biological application.
