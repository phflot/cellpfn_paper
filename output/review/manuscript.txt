                                                           Abstract
                  Single-cell prediction often requires learning from limited labelled context across heterogeneous
              experiments. We developed CellPFN, a prior-data fitted network trained on a procedural
              cellular prior that varies biological signals, donor structure and measurement noise. The
              12.33-million-parameter model uses task-local gene correspondence without persistent gene
              identities and requires no target-task gradient updates. After 384,000 scheduled synthetic
              episodes, CellPFN achieved competitive cell-type classification relative to pretrained tabular
              models, with approximately 90-fold less reported task exposure than TabICLv2. The cellular
              prior improved three pretrained tabular models through continued training, improving one-shot
              balanced accuracy by 5.6–10.8 percentage points. CellPFN also accepted transcriptome-scale
              inputs and supported efficient gene-deletion explanations. The framework further identifies
              neuropathology-associated cellular states and reproducible predictive expression
              signatures across independent cohorts.[O04] These results position a cellular task prior as a
              reusable resource for learning biological inference with limited labelled data.

 1   Single-cell atlases provide increasingly broad reference maps, yet annotating a new experiment
 2   often requires inference from a small labelled set collected under different biological and technical
 3   conditions. More profiled cells do not necessarily supply more independent donors or more varied
 4   experimental settings. In supervised single-cell annotation, increasing donor diversity can contribute
 5   more to generalization than increasing cell count alone 1 . This distinction motivates models that
 6   learn how to interpret limited experimental context across heterogeneous tasks.
 7         Single-cell foundation models such as Geneformer, scGPT, scFoundation, UCE and Transcript-
 8   Former learn reusable representations from large collections of measured profiles 2–6 . Their breadth
 9   supports transfer across biological settings, but large-scale pretraining does not guarantee an advan-
10   tage on each downstream task. Evaluations have found limitations in frozen representations and

                                                               1
11   saturation as pretraining collections expand 7,8 ; recent preprint evidence also shows that normaliza-
12   tion and linear methods can remain competitive across several benchmarks 9 . These findings motivate
13   a complementary question: can learning improve the inference rule that combines a few labelled
14   examples, even when the expression measurements themselves already provide useful features?
15      Meta-learning already supports transfer across heterogeneous single-cell experiments, as demon-
16   strated by MARS 10 . Prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) provide a complementary way to learn
17   inference rules. They are trained across prediction problems drawn from a generative prior, amortiz-
18   ing support-conditioned inference into a forward pass 11 . TabPFN and TabICL demonstrate that
19   synthetic task distributions can produce effective general-purpose tabular learners 12–15 . Recent
20   MapPFN and PerturbPFN work further applies prior-based learning to cellular perturbation predic-
21   tion 16,17 . For transcriptomic classification, the opportunity is to encode the variation encountered
22   across cellular experiments directly into the training prior, while accommodating thousands of
23   sparse features and scarce labels.
24      Single-cell simulators already model count distributions, dependence, donor variation and
25   regulatory dynamics through complementary statistical and mechanistic approaches 18–25 . Learned
26   generative models also support conditional synthesis and augmentation 26,27 . For PFN training,
27   the design objective extends beyond reproducing a reference dataset to generating diverse labelled
28   inference problems. Controlled variation in signal strength, hidden subpopulations, donor effects
29   and measurement noise can expose a learner to experimental configurations without requiring
30   each configuration to be measured and annotated. Synthetic-to-real learning in optical flow and
31   face analysis provides precedents for learning from known simulated structure 28,29 . Nevertheless,
32   simulator fidelity depends on the statistic and scenario being evaluated 30,31 . The relevant test is
33   whether the induced prior teaches inference that transfers to real biological data.
34      Here we introduce CellPFN and its procedural cellular prior. CellPFN learns from synthetic
35   tasks while preserving gene correspondence within each task and omitting persistent gene identities
36   across tasks. We test two consequences of this prior: whether a compact cellular model can
37   achieve competitive biological inference after substantially fewer training episodes than a general
38   tabular model, and whether tasks from the same simulator can improve released tabular models
39   through continued training. We further examine transcriptome-scale inputs and efficient query-gene
40   explanations. The aim is a reusable method for learning from limited labelled biological context,
41   with the prior’s usefulness established through downstream inference rather than simulation scale
42   alone.


43   Results
44   A cellular prior teaches inference from labelled context
45   We constructed a procedural prior over single-cell prediction problems, varying biological differences
46   together with the conditions under which they are observed. The generator samples hierarchical cell
47   identities, distributed expression programs, rare markers, correlated variation and donor-dependent


                                                       2
48   states. Donor composition, study and batch shifts, sequencing depth and overdispersed count
49   sampling produce heterogeneous observations of these latent signals. Different labels and support
50   budgets turn each simulated dataset into multiple inference problems. This makes experimental
51   variation part of the training distribution, with known task labels and controllable nuisance factors.
52   Aggregate statistics from real datasets, including those later evaluated here, informed the prior’s
53   ranges. All examples used to optimize CellPFN’s weights were synthetic.
54      Each problem comprises labelled support cells and unlabelled queries. Genes retain their
55   correspondence between support and query but have no persistent identity across problems. CellPFN
56   compares each query gene with support-derived class summaries, pools the resulting relations through
57   permutation-invariant statistics and integrates this evidence into class scores. This use of unordered
58   features follows the principle of set-based learning 32 , while preserving the within-task correspondence
59   required for expression comparisons. A covariance estimate fitted to the support cells supplies an
60   additional statistical channel when support is sufficient. The pooled evidence produces direct neural
61   class predictions. Prediction on a new task requires labelled context but no gradient updates, and
62   queries do not condition on other query cells.
63      The reference model has 12.33 million trainable parameters and was trained over 384,000
64   scheduled episodes drawn from 12,000 simulated dataset banks. Reused banks supplied distinct task
65   views; 276,828 episodes carried nonzero loss weight. The first 320,000 scheduled episodes used prior
66   v1, followed by 64,000 using the revised v2.2-B prior. For scale, TabICLv2 reports approximately 35
67   million synthetic pretraining datasets 15 , about 90 times CellPFN’s scheduled episode count. This
68   comparison describes training-task exposure across different priors and architectures; it does not
69   measure a matched-compute advantage.

70   Compact CellPFN is competitive across biological inference regimes
71   We evaluated coarse and fine cell-type annotation in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) 33 ,
72   together with cortex annotation within the development collection and on queries from held-out
73   studies in ZEBRA 34 . Each panel used one, ten or 100 labelled support cells per class. Comparators
74   included support-trained statistical classifiers, released tabular models, and logistic classifiers fitted
75   to Geneformer and scGPT embeddings 2,3,15,35,36 . The main comparisons used the same three
76   support draws. PBMC inputs were counts for CellPFN and normalized expression for tabular
77   comparators; the cortex analysis used the exported processed-expression view. Model-specific input
78   routes were retained for the single-cell foundation models; the Geneformer logistic classifier used
79   standardized embeddings, whereas the scGPT classifier used unscaled embeddings.
80      Across the four panels and three support sizes, CellPFN using all available genes reached a
81   mean balanced accuracy of 72.9%. Released TabICLv2 and Kumo large reached 66.8% and 70.1%,
82   respectively, using 2,000 highly variable genes, while cellular continued training brought Kumo large
83   to 72.4%. Geneformer and scGPT with logistic classifiers reached 74.9% and 69.4% through their
84   native input routes. These descriptive averages place the compact cellular model in a competitive
85   range without establishing statistical equivalence or a universal ranking.

86       Support-trained low-rank linear discriminant analysis (LDA) remained strong when labels were
87    abundant. At 100 support cells per class, LDA using all genes reached 84.6% mean balanced accuracy
88    across the four panels, compared with 85.4% for CellPFN; at ten supports, the corresponding values
89    were 75.3% and 79.6%. LDA received processed expression throughout and was not evaluated with
90    one support cell per class.
91       The ranking depended on the biological task and the amount of labelled context. For fine PBMC
92    annotation with one support cell per class, Geneformer reached 68.8% balanced accuracy versus
93    51.1% for CellPFN. For cortex queries from held-out studies with ten support cells per class, CellPFN
94    reached 91.9%, compared with 87.4% for Geneformer and 87.2% for scGPT. Both foundation models
95    likely encountered the exact PBMC cells during unlabelled pretraining; Geneformer also encountered
96    source studies contributing most held-out cortex queries. This exposure does not establish label
97    leakage or explain the performance differences, which also reflect preprocessing and classifier settings.
98    Moreover, model variants were selected on these development panels, and each training arm currently
99    has one training seed. A frozen evaluation on additional, independently held-out cohorts
100   will determine how consistently this competitive performance generalizes.[O01]

101   The cellular prior also improves pretrained tabular models
102   To test whether the prior’s utility extends beyond CellPFN, we continued training released TabICLv2,
103   Kumo small and Kumo large on tasks from the same simulator, using prior v2.2-B throughout
104   cellular adaptation. Each model received 40,000 additional cellular tasks and 10,000 generic replay
105   tasks over 2,500 updates. With 2,000 highly variable genes at evaluation, one-shot balanced accuracy
106   improved by 7.3, 10.8 and 5.6 percentage points, respectively, averaged across the four core panels.
107   Changes at 100 support cells per class were smaller than one percentage point in magnitude. Thus
108   the largest benefits arose where labelled biological context was most limited.
109      A generic-table continued-training control for Kumo small changed mean balanced accuracy
110   by approximately zero across the eight low-support panel–budget combinations, compared with a
111   6.1-point gain for cellular continued training. This comparison supports a contribution from the
112   training distribution, although the controls differed in rows and columns per update and equivalent
113   controls were unavailable for the other architectures. The experiments therefore demonstrate two
114   uses of the cellular prior: training a compact specialist from scratch and adapting already pretrained
115   tabular learners.

116   Gene-anonymous inference extends to transcriptome-scale inputs
117   We next asked whether CellPFN could use more genes than it encountered during training. The
118   largest training tasks contained 8,192 genes, whereas the PBMC and processed cortex evaluations
119   contained 33,538 and 21,158 genes, respectively. The same shared per-gene computations and
120   symmetric aggregation accepted these larger inputs without expanding a learned gene vocabulary.
121   Across the matched benchmark draws, balanced accuracy increased from 71.2% with 2,000 highly
122   variable genes to 73.8% with 8,192, and was 72.9% with all genes. Additional genes therefore

                                                         4
123   remained usable, although retaining every gene did not maximize aggregate accuracy and was not
124   beneficial in every task.
125      Released TabICLv2 declined from 66.8% to 48.3% and Kumo large from 70.1% to 66.4% when
126   moving from 2,000 highly variable genes to all genes. TabPFN-3.5, evaluated under its non-
127   commercial research terms, refused the all-gene cases at its default feature guard 36,37 . These
128   outcomes describe the tested inference configurations, including their column handling; they do not
129   establish a fundamental limit of tabular architectures. CellPFN’s advantage here is the ability to
130   retain transcriptome-scale input under its existing inference procedure.

131   Prior and architecture changes have regime-dependent effects
132   We examined whether explicitly exposing cross-gene structure accounted for CellPFN’s performance.
133   The support-fitted covariance channel supplied both per-gene variance information and low-rank
134   relationships between genes. Later comparisons of separately trained models with no operator,
135   diagonal information alone or the full operator showed gains in several support-rich settings. However,
136   the prespecified operator test did not support its primary claim: an operator-attributable loss
137   exceeded the allowed threshold on a primary task. The later descriptive improvements therefore do
138   not establish the operator as uniformly beneficial, and variation across independent training runs
139   remains unmeasured.
140      Changing the simulated task distribution also produced a tradeoff. Continuation on tasks selected
141   as learnable improved some low-support accuracies but worsened predictive log loss at larger support
142   sizes; the comparison additionally changed the number of cells per update. Simulator diagnostics
143   likewise showed partial agreement with real data rather than uniform fidelity: the prior covered
144   the observed strongest-marker range, while its few-cell discrimination tasks were easier than the
145   real reference. Together, these results motivate judging the prior through transfer and targeted
146   controls, alongside distributional checks, rather than treating greater simulated realism or a stronger
147   statistical channel as sufficient evidence of better inference.

148   Query-gene deletion explains predictions efficiently
149   Gene anonymity still permits gene-level explanations because feature names can be restored after
150   prediction. We developed DeepSets leave-one-gene-out (DS-LOO), which estimates how setting an
151   individual query gene to zero changes the logit margin between the initially predicted class and its
152   runner-up. The support set and fitted covariance remain fixed. In contrast to gradient attribution
153   along an input path 38 , the target is this finite query-side deletion. DS-LOO uses the pooled gene
154   statistics and a linearization of the prediction head, with corrections for changes in depth and
155   detected-gene fraction. It is an approximation to a model intervention, not a simulated biological
156   gene knockout.
157      Against exact deletions in six evaluated cases and two model states, the case-level medians of
158   within-cell rank correlation ranged from 0.979 to 0.998. For the reference model, case-level corrected
159   relative L2 errors ranged from 0.5% to 4.9%. These checks used selected genes and correctly

                                                         5
160   classified cells in full precision. Under matched load, computing all-gene explanations
161   cost approximately 10–13 single-gene exact forward passes. For the PBMC example,
162   DS-LOO took 67.9 seconds; the corresponding 61-hour exhaustive-deletion runtime was
163   extrapolated from individual forward timings rather than measured directly. Shared-
164   GPU timings remain provisional.[O05]
165      Despite the absence of gene-name embeddings, IL7R ranked second for CD4 T cells and NKG7
166   seventh for natural killer cells in the evaluated class-margin rankings. Removing each PBMC
167   query’s 50 most influential genes reversed its margin against the original runner-up class in 45.0%
168   of evaluated cells, compared with 0.3% for random expressed genes. This supports faithfulness to
169   the classifier’s decisions. It does not show that the same deletions would change biological identity
170   or predict experimental perturbation responses.

171   A provisional application examines neuropathology-associated expression
172   We extended the framework to donor-level neuropathology contrasts in ROSMAP-
173   derived single-nucleus data 39 . In the exported analysis with case and control support
174   matched within sex, microglial predictions distinguished the CERAD 4-versus-1 donor
175   groups with an AUC of 0.745 and a reported corrected 95% repeated-split interval of
176   0.700–0.790. This result remains provisional pending completion of the donor-label
177   audit and a frozen rerun. It measures association with neuropathology, not prospective
178   diagnosis or clinical utility.[O02]
179      Applying query-gene deletion to the corrected disease analysis and testing the
180   findings in independent cohorts will test whether this approach can connect donor-level
181   prediction to reproducible cellular expression signatures.[O03]
