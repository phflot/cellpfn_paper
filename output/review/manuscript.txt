                                                        Abstract

                 Single-cell prediction often requires learning from limited labelled context across heteroge-
             neous experiments. We developed CellPFN, a prior-data fitted network trained on a procedural
             cellular prior varying biological signals, donor structure and measurement noise. The 12.33-
             million-parameter model uses task-local gene correspondence without persistent gene identities or
             target-task gradient updates. After 384,000 scheduled synthetic episodes, CellPFN reached 72.9%
             mean balanced accuracy across four cell-typing panels and three support budgets, compared
             with 66.8–70.1% for released TabICLv2 and Kumo large under their tested input configurations.
             Cellular continued training improved three tabular models’ one-shot balanced accuracy by 5.6–
             10.8 percentage points. Ablations revealed support-dependent benefits and tradeoffs. CellPFN
             also accepted transcriptome-scale inputs and yielded gene-deletion explanations recovering estab-
             lished cell-type markers. The framework further identifies neuropathology-associated
             cellular states and reproducible predictive expression signatures across independent
             cohorts.[O04] These results support cellular task priors for learning biological inference with
             limited labelled data.

1   Single-cell atlases provide increasingly broad reference maps, yet annotating a new experiment
2   often requires inference from a small labelled set collected under different biological and technical
3   conditions. More profiled cells do not necessarily supply more independent donors or more varied
4   experimental settings. In supervised single-cell annotation, increasing donor diversity can contribute
5   more to generalization than increasing cell count alone 1 . This distinction motivates models that
6   learn how to interpret limited experimental context across heterogeneous tasks.
7         Single-cell foundation models such as Geneformer, scGPT, scFoundation, UCE and Transcript-
8   Former learn reusable representations from large collections of measured profiles 2–6 . Their breadth


                                                            1
 9   supports transfer across biological settings, but large-scale pretraining does not guarantee an advan-
10   tage on each downstream task. Evaluations have found limitations in frozen representations and
11   saturation as pretraining collections expand 7,8 ; recent preprint evidence also shows that normaliza-
12   tion and linear methods can remain competitive across several benchmarks 9 . These findings motivate
13   a complementary question: can learning improve the inference rule that combines a few labelled
14   examples, even when the expression measurements themselves already provide useful features?
15      Meta-learning already supports transfer across heterogeneous single-cell experiments, as demon-
16   strated by MARS 10 . Prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) provide a complementary way to learn
17   inference rules. They are trained across prediction problems drawn from a generative prior, amortiz-
18   ing support-conditioned inference into a forward pass 11 . TabPFN and TabICL demonstrate that
19   synthetic task distributions can produce effective general-purpose tabular learners 12–15 . Recent
20   MapPFN and PerturbPFN work further applies prior-based learning to cellular perturbation predic-
21   tion 16,17 . For transcriptomic classification, the opportunity is to encode the variation encountered
22   across cellular experiments directly into the training prior, while accommodating thousands of
23   sparse features and scarce labels.
24      Single-cell simulators already model count distributions, dependence, donor variation and
25   regulatory dynamics through complementary statistical and mechanistic approaches 18–25 . Learned
26   generative models also support conditional synthesis and augmentation 26,27 . For PFN training,
27   the design objective extends beyond reproducing a reference dataset to generating diverse labelled
28   inference problems. Controlled variation in signal strength, hidden subpopulations, donor effects
29   and measurement noise can expose a learner to experimental configurations without requiring
30   each configuration to be measured and annotated. Synthetic-to-real learning in optical flow and
31   face analysis provides precedents for learning from known simulated structure 28,29 . Nevertheless,
32   simulator fidelity depends on the statistic and scenario being evaluated 30,31 . The relevant test is
33   whether the induced prior teaches inference that transfers to real biological data.
34      Here we introduce CellPFN and its procedural cellular prior. CellPFN preserves gene corre-
35   spondence within each task without persistent gene identities across tasks. We test whether a
36   compact model can achieve competitive biological inference with limited synthetic training exposure,
37   and whether the same simulator can improve released tabular models through continued training.
38   Quantitative ablations examine which architectural and task-distribution choices help, where they
39   fail, and how simulator statistics relate to transfer. We further evaluate transcriptome-scale inputs
40   and query-gene explanations, grounding the prior’s utility in inference from limited labelled biological
41   context.


42   Results
43   A cellular prior teaches inference from labelled context
44   We constructed a procedural prior over single-cell prediction problems, varying biological signals
45   together with their observation conditions. The generator samples hierarchical identities, distributed


                                                        2
46   expression programs, rare markers, correlated variation and donor-dependent states. Donor com-
47   position, study and batch shifts, sequencing depth and overdispersed count sampling produce
48   heterogeneous observations. Varying labels and support budgets turns each simulated dataset into
49   multiple inference problems with known labels and controllable nuisance factors. Aggregate statistics
50   from real datasets, including those evaluated here, informed the prior’s ranges. All examples used
51   to optimize CellPFN’s weights were synthetic.
52      Each problem comprises labelled support cells and unlabelled queries. Genes retain their
53   correspondence within a task but have no persistent identity across tasks. CellPFN compares
54   each query gene with support-derived class summaries, pools these relations through permutation-
55   invariant statistics 32 and integrates them into direct neural class predictions. A covariance estimate
56   fitted to the support supplies an additional statistical channel when support is sufficient. Prediction
57   requires no gradient updates, and queries do not condition on other query cells.
58      The reference model has 12.33 million trainable parameters and received 384,000 scheduled
59   episodes from 12,000 simulated dataset banks; 276,828 episodes carried nonzero loss weight. Reused
60   banks supplied distinct task views. The first 320,000 episodes used prior v1 and the remaining
61   64,000 used v2.2-B. TabICLv2 reports approximately 35 million synthetic pretraining datasets 15 ,
62   about 90 times CellPFN’s scheduled episode count (Supplementary Table S2). These different
63   exposure units contextualize training scale but do not establish a matched-compute advantage.

64   Compact CellPFN is competitive across biological inference regimes
65   We evaluated coarse and fine peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) annotation 33 , cortex
66   annotation within the development collection, and cortex queries from held-out ZEBRA studies 34 .
67   Each panel used one, ten or 100 labelled support cells per class with three matched support draws.
68   Comparators included statistical classifiers, released tabular models, and logistic classifiers on
69   Geneformer-V2 and scGPT embeddings 2,3,15,35–37 . PBMC inputs were counts for CellPFN and
70   normalized expression for tabular comparators; cortex used processed expression. Foundation models
71   retained their native input routes, with standardized Geneformer and unscaled scGPT embeddings.
72      Across the four panels and three support sizes, all-gene CellPFN reached 72.9% mean balanced
73   accuracy, compared with 66.8% for released TabICLv2 and 70.1% for Kumo large using 2k highly
74   variable genes (HVGs; Table 1). Geneformer reached 74.9% through its native input route. These
75   descriptive averages place CellPFN in a competitive range without establishing equivalence or a
76   universal ranking. Supplementary Table S1 reports panel-specific means and three-draw ranges.
77      Conventional low-rank linear discriminant analysis (LDA) remained strong with abundant labels:
78   at 100 supports per class, its four-panel mean was 84.6%, compared with 85.4% for CellPFN; at ten
79   supports, the means were 75.3% and 79.6%. LDA used processed expression and was not evaluated
80   at one support.
81      Task-specific differences were larger than the overall differences. For fine PBMC annotation
82   at one support, Geneformer reached 68.8% versus CellPFN’s 51.1%; for held-out cortex at ten
83   supports, CellPFN reached 91.9% versus Geneformer’s 87.4%. Both foundation models likely


                                                       3
84    encountered the PBMC cells during unlabelled pretraining. Geneformer-V2 corpus records overlap
85    studies supplying 92.6% of held-out cortex queries, an upper bound on exact-cell overlap. This
86    evidence does not establish label exposure or explain the ranking (Table 1). Model variants were
87    selected on these development panels, with one training seed per arm. A frozen evaluation
88    on additional, independently held-out cohorts will determine how consistently this
89    competitive performance generalizes.[O01]
90       Finer PBMC distinctions remained difficult. At 8,192 HVGs, a separate evaluation gave one-shot
91    balanced accuracies of 62.3% for eight coarse types, 53.9% for 20 fine types and 32.4% across eight
92    harder tasks. The latter separated sibling CD4, CD8, B, NK or broader T-cell subtypes, or all 35
93    fine-grained labels; their means rose to 63.9% and 80.2% at ten and 100 supports (Supplementary
94    Table S9). Nine CD4 subtypes yielded 23.4% and 79.6% at one and 100 supports. This analysis
95    used ten support draws at one/ten supports and five at 100. The harder tasks were defined post
96    hoc; they are not a prespecified binary difficulty stratum, and the CellPFN export supplies means
97    without draw-level intervals.

98    The cellular prior also improves pretrained tabular models
99    The cellular prior also improved already pretrained tabular learners. We continued training released
100   TabICLv2, Kumo small and Kumo large using v2.2-B throughout, with 40,000 cellular tasks and
101   10,000 generic replay tasks over 2,500 updates per model. At 2k HVGs, one-shot balanced accuracy
102   improved by 7.3, 10.8 and 5.6 percentage points, respectively, averaged over four panels (Table 1).
103   Changes at 100 supports were below one point in magnitude. Benefits were not uniform: fine PBMC
104   accuracy for TabICLv2 at ten supports fell from 73.8% to 64.6%, with all three support draws
105   worsening (Supplementary Table S2).
106      Cellular continued training of Kumo small improved accuracy by 6.1 points across eight low-
107   support panel–budget combinations, compared with approximately zero change after generic-table
108   continued training. Two further cellular task streams yielded gains of 5.7 and 5.2 points with the
109   same model seed. The control differed in rows and columns per update, so task content was not
110   isolated under matched data volume. Adaptation could also reduce generic-table performance:
111   TabICLv2’s one-shot mean balanced accuracy across four generic tables and five split seeds fell from
112   70.9% to 64.0% (Supplementary Table S2).

113   Gene-anonymous inference extends to transcriptome-scale inputs
114   CellPFN accepted more genes than it encountered during training: the widest training tasks
115   contained 8,192 genes, whereas PBMC and cortex evaluations contained 33,538 and 21,158. Shared
116   per-gene computations and symmetric aggregation required no expanded vocabulary. Across the
117   three benchmark draws, balanced accuracy was 71.2% with 2k HVGs, 73.8% with 8,192 and 72.9%
118   with all genes (Table 2). A separate twenty-draw analysis resolved task-specific effects: all genes
119   versus 2k HVGs improved held-out cortex accuracy at ten supports by 7.4 points (95% interval,
120   5.3–9.6), but reduced one-shot PBMC accuracy by 3.3 points for coarse and 2.0 points for fine labels

                                                       4
121   (Supplementary Table S3). These paired intervals quantify support-draw variation, not training-run
122   variation.
123      Released TabICLv2 declined from 66.8% to 48.3% and Kumo large from 70.1% to 66.4% when
124   moving from 2k HVGs to all genes. TabPFN-3.5 refused the all-gene cases at its default feature
125   guard 37,38 . These are outcomes of the tested column-handling configurations, not fundamental
126   architectural limits. Random-gene controls and alternative input views are reported in Supplementary
127   Tables S3 and S9.

128   Prior and architecture changes have regime-dependent effects
129   The support-fitted covariance channel had substantial but regime-dependent effects. On fine
130   PBMC annotation at 100 supports, separately trained models with no operator, diagonal variance
131   information or the full operator reached 82.2%, 85.7% and 89.0% balanced accuracy. Full versus
132   no operator gained 6.81 points (95% paired interval, 6.36–7.26); full versus diagonal gained 3.37
133   points (3.06–3.68), over twenty support draws with one training run per arm (Supplementary
134   Table S4). Nevertheless, the earlier prespecified test failed: the full model lost 1.56 points against
135   the no-operator model on coarse PBMC at ten supports, exceeding the allowed 1.50-point harm
136   threshold. Later descriptive gains do not overturn that outcome.
137      A ten-candidate prior screen identified learnable-task filtering as the only change meeting its
138   retention rule on the Kumo-small proxy (Supplementary Table S5). After 10,000 cellular tasks,
139   low-support accuracy improved by 1.95 points, with gains of 1.63, 1.62 and 2.61 across three task
140   streams sharing one model seed. No prior-content modification demonstrated benefit. Whole-study
141   gene absence, for example, gained 0.90 points but failed its predictive-log-loss guard, worsening
142   negative log likelihood (NLL) by 0.099 against a 0.03 limit. Other outcomes included neutral and
143   unresolved cases; they do not establish that the corresponding mechanisms are irrelevant.
144      Learnable-task continuation also improved CellPFN’s one-shot accuracy on all four panels:
145   gains were 1.11, 2.42, 2.56 and 3.49 points for coarse PBMC, fine PBMC, development cortex and
146   held-out cortex, respectively (Supplementary Table S6). The held-out cortex gain had a 95% paired
147   interval of 1.73–5.26 over twenty support draws. At 100 supports, NLL worsened on three panels by
148   0.35–0.63, while improving by 0.05 on fine PBMC. This is a probabilistic-prediction tradeoff, not a
149   calibration-only measurement. Learnable-task continuation processed 1.67 times as many cells per
150   update, precluding attribution to filtering alone.
151      Simulator diagnostics likewise supported partial agreement with real data (Supplementary
152   Table S7). The median strongest-marker absolute log2 fold change was 6.23 under v2.2-B versus
153   7.10 in the real reference and 1.94 under v1. Yet eight-class tasks with ten supports were easier:
154   median chance-adjusted nearest-class-mean accuracy was 0.881 versus 0.775, and 37.5% of synthetic
155   banks exceeded the real maximum. These reference statistics informed prior calibration, and v2.2-B
156   supplied only the final 64,000 CellPFN episodes. They therefore contextualize transfer without
157   establishing that increased realism caused better predictions.

158   Sex prediction varies across evaluation settings
159   CellPFN also predicted sex in ZEBRA, but performance depended on the evaluation setting. On
160   one frozen oligodendrocyte case, it achieved 100% balanced accuracy over 50 query cells and over
161   ten query GSM samples, using 100 support cells per sex. Support and query GSMs were disjoint;
162   GSM identifiers do not guarantee distinct donors. A later positive control restricted studies to those
163   measuring XIST and at least four Y-linked genes. With 8,192 HVGs, mean unit-level AUC was 0.977
164   at four support units per sex and 1.000 at 16, across ten splits; units included age-by-sex sample
165   groups. The broader sex task yielded 0.507 and 0.538 at those budgets (Supplementary Table S9).
166   Study sets and gene universes also differed, so this contrast does not isolate the effect of sex-linked
167   genes. Gene-level attribution of these sex predictions will establish which expression
168   features the model uses; the available results do not yet identify those features.[O06]

169   Query-gene deletion recovers cell-type marker structure
170   Gene anonymity still permits gene-level explanations because feature names can be restored after
171   prediction. DeepSets leave-one-gene-out (DS-LOO) approximates the effect of zeroing one query
172   gene on the logit margin between the predicted class and its original runner-up, holding support
173   and fitted covariance fixed. Unlike gradient attribution along an input path 39 , the target is a finite
174   deletion. One attribution sweep combines pooled gene statistics with head-gradient evaluations and
175   depth/detection corrections, avoiding a separate full-model forward pass per deleted gene. It is not
176   literally one forward pass, and its target is a model intervention rather than a biological knockout.
177      Against exact deletions in six cases and two model states, case-level medians of within-cell rank
178   correlation ranged from 0.979 to 0.998; reference-model relative L2 errors were 0.5–4.9%. Validation
179   used selected genes and correctly classified cells in full precision (Supplementary Table S8). All-gene
180   explanations cost approximately 10–13 single-gene exact forward passes. For the PBMC
181   example, DS-LOO took 67.9 seconds; the corresponding 61-hour exhaustive-deletion
182   runtime was extrapolated from individual forward timings. Shared-GPU timings
183   remain provisional.[O05]
184      The explanations recovered recognizable cell-type markers without gene-name embeddings. In
185   PBMC class-margin rankings at 100 supports, B-cell markers CD79A and MS4A1 ranked first and
186   second; IL7R ranked second for CD4 T cells, CD8A second for CD8 T cells, and GNLY /NKG7
187   second/seventh for natural killer cells 33,40 . In development cortex, oligodendrocyte-associated
188   PLP1 ranked eighth for glia, neuronal SYT1 seventh for neural cells, and endothelial CLDN5
189   sixteenth for vascular cells 41 . These are selected lineage-associated matches, not an enrichment test
190   (Supplementary Table S8).
191      Removing each PBMC query’s 50 highest-ranked genes reversed its original runner-up margin in
192   45.0% of cells, versus 0.3% for random expressed genes. Class-level top-20 overlap across support
193   configurations was 0.43–0.56, indicating context sensitivity. Thus the rankings explain classifier
194   decisions without establishing perturbation responses or universal marker recovery.

195   A provisional application examines neuropathology-associated expression
196   In a provisional donor-level application to ROSMAP-derived single-nucleus data 42 ,
197   microglial predictions distinguished CERAD 4-versus-1 neuropathology groups with an
198   AUC of 0.745 and a reported corrected 95% repeated-split interval of 0.700–0.790. Case
199   and control support was matched within sex (Supplementary Table S9). The result
200   awaits a donor-label audit and frozen rerun; it measures association, not prospective
201   diagnosis or clinical utility.[O02]
202      Applying query-gene deletion to the corrected disease analysis and testing the
203   findings in independent cohorts will test whether this approach can connect donor-level
204   prediction to reproducible cellular expression signatures.[O03]
