Single-cell prediction often requires learning from limited labelled context across heterogeneous experiments. We developed CellPFN, a prior-data fitted network trained on a procedural cellular prior that varies biological signals, donor structure and measurement noise. The 12.33-million-parameter model uses task-local gene correspondence without persistent gene identities and requires no target-task gradient updates. After 384,000 scheduled synthetic episodes, CellPFN achieved competitive cell-type classification relative to pretrained tabular models, with approximately 90-fold less reported task exposure than TabICLv2. The cellular prior improved three pretrained tabular models through continued training, particularly when labels were scarce. CellPFN also accepted transcriptome-scale inputs and supported efficient gene-deletion explanations. \unresolvedclaim{O04}{The framework further identifies neuropathology-associated cellular states and reproducible predictive expression signatures across independent cohorts.} These results position a cellular task prior as a reusable resource for learning biological inference with limited labelled data.
