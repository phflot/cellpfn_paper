\subsection*{A cellular prior teaches inference from labelled context}

% P-R01 | fig1__facts: simulator.*; notes/result_summary.md qualitative anchors
We constructed a procedural prior over single-cell prediction problems, varying biological signals together with their observation conditions. The simulator delivers PyTorch-compatible cell streams and reusable biological datasets to both learning routes (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). The generator samples hierarchical identities, distributed expression programs, rare markers, correlated variation and donor-dependent states. Donor composition, study and batch shifts, sequencing depth and overdispersed count sampling produce heterogeneous observations. Varying labels and support budgets turns each simulated dataset into multiple inference problems with known labels and controllable nuisance factors. Aggregate statistics from real datasets, including those evaluated here, informed the prior's ranges. All examples used to optimize CellPFN's weights were synthetic.

% P-R02 | fig1__facts: episode/input/architecture; current neural-only contract
Each problem comprises labelled support cells and unlabelled queries. Genes retain their correspondence within a task but have no persistent identity across tasks. CellPFN compares each query gene with support-derived class summaries, pools these relations through permutation-invariant statistics\cite{zaheer2017deepsets} and integrates them into direct neural class predictions. A covariance estimate fitted to the support supplies an additional statistical channel when support is sufficient. Prediction requires no gradient updates, and queries do not condition on other query cells.

% P-R03 | R01; T01-T07 | external numerator: qu2026tabiclv2 section4.1
The reference model has 12.33 million trainable parameters and received 384,000 scheduled episodes from 12,000 simulated dataset banks; 276,828 episodes carried nonzero loss weight. Reused banks supplied distinct task views. TabICLv2 reports approximately 35 million synthetic pretraining datasets\cite{qu2026tabiclv2}, about 90 times CellPFN's scheduled episode count (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s2}). These different exposure units contextualize training scale but do not establish a matched-compute advantage.

\subsection*{Compact CellPFN is competitive across biological inference regimes}

% P-R04 | fig4 panel definitions; R02-R12; processed Zebra primary view
Across two biological collections, we evaluated eight-class coarse and twenty-class fine peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) annotation\cite{hao2021multimodal}, and four-class ZEBRA cortex annotation in development and held-study panels\cite{flotho2024zebra}. The cortex classes were glia, immune, neural and vascular cells. Each panel used one, ten or 100 labelled support cells per class with three matched support draws. Comparators included statistical classifiers, released tabular models, and logistic classifiers on Geneformer-V2 and scGPT embeddings\cite{theodoris2023geneformer,chen2026geneformer,cui2024scgpt,qu2026tabiclv2,nvidia2026kumo,jaeger2026tabpfn35}. Each model retained its declared input route (Methods; Fig.~\ref{fig:performance}a--d).

% P-R05 | R02-R07: equal-weight descriptive averages across12 task/budget cells
Across the four panels and three support sizes, all-gene CellPFN reached 72.9\% mean balanced accuracy, compared with 66.8\% for released TabICLv2 and 70.1\% for Kumo large using 2k highly variable genes (HVGs; Table~\ref{table:overview}). Geneformer reached 74.9\% through its native input route. These averages indicate competitive performance, without establishing equivalence or universal superiority. Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:s2} and Table~\ref{tab:s1} reports panel-specific means and three-draw ranges.

% P-R05b | R54-R57; same cases/draws, model-specific representations
Conventional low-rank linear discriminant analysis (LDA) remained strong with abundant labels: at 100 supports per class, its four-panel mean was 84.6\%, compared with 85.4\% for CellPFN; at ten supports, the means were 75.3\% and 79.6\%. LDA used processed expression and was not evaluated at one support.

% P-R06 | R08-R12; supp_exposure__study_exposure/panel_chances; notes/geneformer_exposure_audit.md; O01
Performance varied substantially across tasks. For fine PBMC annotation at one support, Geneformer reached 68.8\% versus CellPFN's 51.1\%; for held-out cortex at ten supports, CellPFN reached 91.9\% versus Geneformer's 87.4\%. Both foundation models likely encountered the PBMC cells during unlabelled pretraining. Geneformer-V2 corpus records overlap studies supplying 92.6\% of held-out cortex queries, an upper bound on exact-cell overlap. This evidence does not establish label exposure or explain the ranking (Table~\ref{table:overview}). Model variants were selected on these development panels, with one training seed per arm. \unresolvedclaim{O01}{A frozen evaluation on additional, independently held-out cohorts will determine how consistently this competitive performance generalizes.}

% P-R06b | X89-X169 | requested HVG caps; post hoc PBMC and cortex tasks, summary-only CellPFN
Finer PBMC distinctions remained difficult. With up to 8,192 HVGs, a separate evaluation gave one-shot balanced accuracies of 62.3\% for eight coarse types, 53.9\% for 20 fine types and 32.4\% across eight harder tasks. The latter separated sibling CD4, CD8, B, NK or broader T-cell subtypes, or all 35 fine-grained labels; their means rose to 63.9\% and 80.2\% at ten and 100 supports (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s9}). Nine CD4 subtypes yielded 23.4\% and 79.6\% at one and 100 supports. This analysis used ten support draws at one/ten supports and five at 100. These post hoc tasks are not a prespecified difficulty stratum; their means lack draw-level intervals. Two finer cortical tasks yielded one-shot accuracies of 67.8\% for seven development labels and 52.5\% for three held-study neural labels (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s9}). CellPFN handled twenty and 35 PBMC labels despite training with at most eight classes.


\subsection*{The cellular prior also improves pretrained tabular models}

% P-R09 | T08-T10; R20-R25; R67-R69 | adaptation is not lifetime exposure
The cellular prior also improved pretrained tabular learners (Fig.~\ref{fig:performance}e--g). We continued training released TabICLv2, Kumo small and Kumo large using the cellular prior throughout, with 40,000 cellular tasks and 10,000 generic replay tasks over 2,500 updates per model. At 2k HVGs, one-shot balanced accuracy improved by 7.3, 10.8 and 5.6 percentage points, respectively, averaged over four panels (Table~\ref{table:overview}). Changes at 100 supports were below one point in magnitude. Benefits were not uniform: fine PBMC accuracy for TabICLv2 at ten supports fell from 73.8\% to 64.6\%, with all three support draws worsening (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s2}).

% P-R10 | R26-R27; R70-R73; R158-R159 | unmatched table dimensions
Cellular continued training of Kumo small improved accuracy by 6.1 points across eight low-support panel--budget combinations, compared with approximately zero change after generic-table continued training. Two further cellular task streams yielded gains of 5.7 and 5.2 points with the same model seed. The control differed in rows and columns per update, so task content was not isolated under matched data volume. Adaptation could also reduce generic-table performance: TabICLv2's one-shot mean balanced accuracy across four generic tables and five split seeds fell from 70.9\% to 64.0\% (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s2}).

\subsection*{Gene-anonymous inference extends to transcriptome-scale inputs}

% P-R07 | R41-R44; R13,R14,R02; R58-R66 | separate three- and twenty-draw analyses
CellPFN processed 33,538 PBMC and 21,158 cortex genes after training with at most 8,192. Shared per-gene computations and symmetric aggregation required no expanded vocabulary. Across the three benchmark draws, balanced accuracy was 71.2\% with 2k HVGs, 73.8\% with 8,192 and 72.9\% with all genes (Table~\ref{table:gene_ablation}). A separate twenty-draw analysis resolved task-specific effects: all genes versus 2k HVGs improved held-out cortex accuracy at ten supports by 7.4 points (95\% interval, 5.3--9.6), but reduced one-shot PBMC accuracy by 3.3 points for coarse and 2.0 points for fine labels (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:s3} and Table~\ref{tab:s3}). These paired intervals quantify support-draw variation, not training-run variation.

% P-R08 | R03,R04,R15,R16,R19; runtime/configuration claim rather than impossibility
Released TabICLv2 declined from 66.8\% to 48.3\% and Kumo large from 70.1\% to 66.4\% when moving from 2k HVGs to all genes. TabPFN-3.5 refused the all-gene cases at its default feature guard\cite{jaeger2026tabpfn35,priorlabs2026tabpfn35card}. These are outcomes of the tested column-handling configurations, not fundamental architectural limits. Random-gene controls and alternative input views are reported in Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:s3} and~\ref{tab:s9}.

\subsection*{Prior and architecture changes have regime-dependent effects}

% P-R11 | R74-R87 | frozen test and later twenty-draw decomposition are distinct
The support-fitted covariance channel had substantial but regime-dependent effects. On fine PBMC annotation at 100 supports, separately trained models with no operator, diagonal variance information or the full operator reached 82.2\%, 85.7\% and 89.0\% balanced accuracy. Full versus no operator gained 6.81 points (95\% paired interval, 6.36--7.26); full versus diagonal gained 3.37 points (3.06--3.68), over twenty support draws with one training run per arm (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:s4} and Table~\ref{tab:s4}). The prespecified operator test did not meet its retention criterion: the full model lost 1.56 points against the no-operator model on coarse PBMC at ten supports, exceeding the allowed 1.50-point harm threshold. The descriptive gains do not overturn that outcome.

% P-R12 | R88-R101 | ten-candidate proxy campaign and failed NLL guard
A ten-candidate prior screen identified task-mixture restriction as the only change meeting its retention rule on the Kumo-small proxy (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:s5} and Table~\ref{tab:s5}). After 10,000 cellular tasks, low-support accuracy improved by 1.95 points, with gains of 1.63, 1.62 and 2.61 across three task streams sharing one model seed. No prior-content modification demonstrated benefit. Whole-study gene absence, for example, gained 0.90 points but failed its predictive-log-loss guard, worsening negative log likelihood (NLL) by 0.099 against a 0.03 limit. Other outcomes included neutral and unresolved cases; they do not establish that the corresponding mechanisms are irrelevant.

% P-R12b | R102-R128 | supplied twenty-draw paired intervals and unequal exposure
The restricted mixture also improved CellPFN's one-shot accuracy on all four panels: gains were 1.11, 2.42, 2.56 and 3.49 points for coarse PBMC, fine PBMC, development cortex and held-out cortex, respectively (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:s5} and Table~\ref{tab:s6}). The held-out cortex gain had a 95\% paired interval of 1.73--5.26 over twenty support draws. At 100 supports, NLL worsened on three panels by 0.35--0.63, while improving by 0.05 on fine PBMC. The restriction replaced an approximately 80\% metadata-task share with 75\% cell-type and 25\% donor-identity tasks and excluded signed-covariance banks. It also processed 1.67 times as many cells per update. The accuracy and log-loss changes therefore reflect a combined task-mixture intervention.

% P-R12c | R129-R153 | public-reference diagnostics; calibration is not independent validation
Simulator diagnostics likewise supported partial agreement with real data (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:s1} and Table~\ref{tab:s7}). The median strongest-marker absolute log2 fold change was 6.23 under the cellular prior versus 7.10 in the real reference and 1.94 under the baseline prior. Yet eight-class tasks with ten supports were easier: median chance-adjusted nearest-class-mean accuracy was 0.881 versus 0.775, and 37.5\% of synthetic banks exceeded the real maximum. These reference statistics informed prior calibration, and the cellular prior supplied only the final 64,000 CellPFN episodes. They therefore contextualize transfer without establishing that increased realism caused better predictions.

\subsection*{Sex prediction varies across evaluation settings}

% P-R18 | X01-X40 | single GSM-disjoint case versus distinct unit-level controls
CellPFN also predicted sex in ZEBRA, but performance depended on the evaluation setting. On one frozen oligodendrocyte case, it achieved 100\% balanced accuracy over 50 query cells and over ten query GSM samples, using 100 support cells per sex. Support and query GSMs were disjoint; GSM identifiers do not guarantee distinct donors. A later positive control restricted studies to those measuring \textit{XIST} and at least four Y-linked genes. With up to 8,192 HVGs, mean unit-level AUC was 0.977 at four support units per sex and 1.000 at 16, across ten splits; units included age-by-sex sample groups. The broader sex task yielded 0.507 and 0.538 at those budgets (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s9}). Study sets and gene universes also differed, so this contrast does not isolate the effect of sex-linked genes. \unresolvedclaim{O06}{Gene-level attribution of these sex predictions will establish which expression features the model uses; the available results do not yet identify those features.}

\subsection*{Query-gene deletion recovers cell-type marker structure}

% P-R13 | fig5 contract and comparison with IG; R28-R31
Gene anonymity permits gene-level explanations because names can be restored after prediction (Fig.~\ref{fig:biological-explanations}). DeepSets leave-one-gene-out (DS-LOO) approximates the effect of zeroing one query gene on the logit margin between the predicted class and its original runner-up, holding support and fitted covariance fixed. Unlike gradient attribution along an input path\cite{sundararajan2017ig}, the target is a finite deletion. One attribution sweep combines pooled gene statistics and operator scalars with head-gradient evaluations and depth/detection corrections, avoiding a separate full-model forward pass per deleted gene. It is not literally one forward pass, and its target is a model intervention rather than a biological knockout.

% P-R14 | R28-R35 | fidelity versus cost; exact-all-gene hours are extrapolated
Against exact deletions in six cases and two model states, case-level medians of within-cell rank correlation ranged from 0.979 to 0.998; reference-model relative L2 errors were 0.5--4.9\%. Validation used selected genes and correctly classified cells in full precision (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:s8} and Table~\ref{tab:s8}). \unresolvedclaim{O05}{All-gene explanations cost approximately 10--13 single-gene exact forward passes. For the PBMC example, DS-LOO took 67.9 seconds; the corresponding 61-hour exhaustive-deletion runtime was extrapolated from individual forward timings. Shared-GPU timings remain provisional.}

% P-R15 | X47-X88; R36,R37,R46,R47; R154-R157 | selected marker matches, not enrichment
The explanations recovered recognizable cell-type markers without gene-name embeddings. In PBMC class-margin rankings at 100 supports, B-cell markers \textit{CD79A} and \textit{MS4A1} ranked first and second; \textit{IL7R} ranked second for CD4 T cells, \textit{CD8A} second for CD8 T cells, and \textit{GNLY}/\textit{NKG7} second/seventh for natural killer cells\cite{hao2021multimodal,schafflick2020leukocytes}. In development cortex, oligodendrocyte-associated \textit{PLP1} ranked eighth for glia, neuronal \textit{SYT1} seventh for neural cells, and endothelial \textit{CLDN5} sixteenth for vascular cells\cite{gong2025stereoseq}. These are selected lineage-associated matches, not an enrichment test (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s8}).

% P-R15b | R36,R37,R46,R47; R154-R157 | finite deletion fidelity and support sensitivity
Removing each PBMC query's 50 highest-ranked genes reversed its original runner-up margin in 45.0\% of cells, versus 0.3\% for random expressed genes. Class-level top-20 overlap across support configurations was 0.43--0.56, indicating context sensitivity. Thus the rankings explain classifier decisions without establishing perturbation responses or universal marker recovery.
